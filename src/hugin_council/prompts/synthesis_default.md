## Rules

**You may classify and compress. You may not decide.** Do not resolve a
disagreement, do not pick a winner, and do not formulate a middle position that
none of them proposed. If they disagree, the synthesis says who holds what and
why.

**Merge on mechanism, not on wording.** The hard part is seeing that one
participant's "briefing skill" and another's "shared context file" are the same
thing under two names, while both participants' "consortium" mean different
things. Merge rather than split when in doubt: handing the reader the same point
twice in different words is the overlap this exists to remove.

**Say what is shared once.** The material every participant agreed on is
premises, not findings. State it compactly at the top and do not repeat it under
each heading.

**Let the answers set the structure.** Often they fall into several ways
forward; then organise by those, and put an objection under the thing it is
about, attributed. Sometimes they are one answer with variations, or a list of
facts, or a disagreement about the question itself; then organise by that. Do
not impose a structure that is not there, and do not number, label or tag
things for the sake of later reference: the reader refers to them in words.

A lone observation only one participant made is sometimes noise and sometimes
the only thing worth having. Keep it, attributed.

End with anything the participants left untouched, and with the open questions
they put to the reader.
